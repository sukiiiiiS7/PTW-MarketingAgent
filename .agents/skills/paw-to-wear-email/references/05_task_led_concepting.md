# Task-Led Email Concepting

## Core rule

Every email must be conceived from the **current task**, not from a historical email example.

The approved examples are a visual/layout reference library only. They show what Paw to Wear email can look like; they do not define what a new email should say, how many sections it should contain, or which narrative structure it should follow.

## Decision priority

Use this order when deciding what to create:

1. **Current user brief** — the specific goal, audience/context, product/collection, desired action, supplied assets/links, and any explicit constraints.
2. **Current Paw to Wear Brand Brief** — positioning, voice, message direction, CTA family, product truth, proof, and brand constraints.
3. **Verified task-local facts / Product Truth** — only facts that are safe to state.
4. **Email channel principles** — clarity, subject/preheader roles, hierarchy, responsive HTML, and Klaviyo runtime requirements.
5. **Approved examples** — visual grammar only.

Never reverse this order.

## Concept from first principles

Before drafting, determine:

- What is the single job of this email?
- What should the reader understand, feel, or do after reading it?
- What is the most relevant message for this exact task?
- What objection, uncertainty, desire, product detail, story, or proof actually helps this task?
- What information is unnecessary even if it appeared in an approved example?
- What is the simplest section sequence that carries the idea clearly?
- What should the reader **see** at each important beat, and what visual rhythm best supports that sequence?
- Which parts should be image-first, image+text, dynamic-product, proof/card, or text-only?
- If final images are not supplied, which placeholders should be created automatically?

Then create the email structure from scratch. For marketing/editorial tasks, concept the visual sequence before expanding body copy. Do not let missing image assets collapse the concept into a text-only newsletter.

## What approved examples MAY influence

Examples may inform only presentation choices such as:

- approximately 600px email canvas;
- generous whitespace and section spacing;
- editorial serif headline treatment;
- sans-serif body treatment;
- restrained warm backgrounds and subtle borders;
- button styling and CTA visual weight;
- card proportions;
- image framing / comparison framing;
- mobile stacking behavior;
- overall density and visual restraint;
- canonical footer appearance (which is separately locked).

## What approved examples MUST NOT influence by default

Do not copy, imitate, or inherit from examples:

- subject lines or preheaders;
- headline wording;
- body copy or phrases;
- narrative angle;
- customer-story framing;
- section order;
- number of sections;
- a welcome/process/story/proof sequence;
- objection framing;
- offer placement;
- CTA wording;
- content hierarchy beyond general visual readability;
- product claims;
- discounts or codes;
- links;
- Klaviyo event-variable paths;
- lifecycle strategy;
- dynamic-content logic.

If the current task does not need a story, process explanation, proof checklist, offer card, collection grid, or brand closing, do not add one merely because an approved example contains it.

## New layouts are expected

The skill is allowed and expected to create new section combinations and new layouts when the task benefits from them, as long as they remain within the current Paw to Wear visual system and email-safe HTML constraints.

Examples are a **style reference, not a template library**.

## Anti-copy QA

Before finalizing, ask:

1. If the approved examples were removed, would this email still make sense as the best response to the current task?
2. Did any section appear mainly because a similar section existed in an example?
3. Did the email inherit a story/proof/process sequence that the task did not require?
4. Are any phrases or CTA labels copied from examples without a task-specific reason?
5. Is the email concept clearly traceable to the current objective and Brand Brief?

If any answer indicates imitation rather than task-led reasoning, reconceive the email.
