# Email Copy Rules

## 1. One email, one primary job

The current task determines the job. Do not inherit the job or narrative from an approved example.

The reader should understand the purpose within the hero/first screenful.

## 2. Subject and preheader are a pair

Do not repeat the same phrase twice.

Preferred relationship:
- Subject creates relevance, curiosity, or clear value appropriate to the task.
- Preheader adds specificity, proof, product scope, or context.

Write both from the current objective and Brand Brief. Do not reuse historical example lines unless the user explicitly asks to reuse them.

## 3. Build hierarchy for the task

There is no mandatory Paw to Wear content sequence.

Possible elements include eyebrow, headline, explanation, visual, product detail, story, proof, offer, dynamic content, and CTA. Select and order only the elements that serve the current email's job.

Avoid abstract message stacking where multiple sections each try to become a new campaign tagline.

## 4. Headline and body roles

When used, the headline may carry the emotional/editorial idea.

Body copy should clarify what the idea means in concrete terms.

Proof should use verified facts, specific details, customer evidence, preview/revision evidence, or product construction rather than unsupported adjectives.

These are functional roles, not a required section order.

## 5. Paw to Wear expression

Use the Brand Brief as the authority. Typical desired qualities:
- warm;
- observant;
- emotionally intelligent;
- restrained;
- fashion-aware;
- specific.

Prefer recognizable details, rituals, personality, relationship cues, and concrete product proof when relevant to the task.

Avoid generic personalized-gift language, excessive `cute`, `precious`, `best friend`, or `keepsake` framing unless the Brand Brief says the context supports it.

## 6. CTA discipline

Use the Brand Brief CTA family, but choose the exact CTA wording for the current task.

For a single email, prefer one primary CTA wording and reuse it only if the button is repeated.

Do not copy CTA labels from approved examples merely because they fit the layout.

## 7. Promotion

Promotion placement follows the current objective.

If the task is not offer-led, value/product/proof should normally remain more prominent than discount language.

If the task is explicitly offer-led, the offer may lead. Still avoid unnecessary all caps, repeated urgency, and stacked promotional slogans unless explicitly requested.

## 8. Length

Length follows the job and evidence available.

Do not make an email long merely to fill components or resemble an approved example.

## 9. Closing

A brand closing is optional, not mandatory. Use it only when the current concept benefits from closure.

Do not add a closing slogan simply because historical emails did.

## 10. Write with the visual, not around it

For image-led sections, shorten adjacent copy and let the visual carry what it can show. Do not write the full explanation first and then add a placeholder as decoration.

For marketing/editorial tasks, if the draft becomes several consecutive paragraphs or text-only sections, check whether product imagery, embroidery details, lifestyle imagery, comparison, process visuals, or proof imagery should replace part of the prose.

Near-checkout and operational emails may intentionally remain concise and text-light without extra static imagery.
