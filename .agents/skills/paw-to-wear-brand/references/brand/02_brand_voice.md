# Brand Voice

## Derived operating guidance for AI writing

**Status: DERIVED.**  
The following rules translate the canonical keywords into repeatable writing behavior. They are not replacement brand statements.

### Do

- Be warm without sounding gushy.
- Be emotional through specific moments, behaviors, rituals, and details.
- Prefer understated confidence to exaggerated claims.
- Sound fashion-aware, not merchandise-like.
- Let craftsmanship and product details carry part of the emotional meaning.
- Use clean, simple language.
- Connect a specific pet to everyday identity and routine.
- Use “story” when there is an actual story, meaning, ritual, memory, or identity behind the piece.
- Keep the pet relationship central without forcing sentiment into every sentence.

### Avoid

- “Pawfect,” “fur baby,” and other cliché pet-copy unless a channel/community context deliberately calls for it.
- Cheap personalization language.
- Generic Etsy/gift-shop language.
- Excessive emotional adjectives.
- Stacking several slogans/taglines that make the reader decode the message.
- Empty luxury language unsupported by product truth.
- Overstating memorial/keepsake framing when the task is everyday lifestyle.
- Treating every customer as a memorial buyer.

### Preferred emotional register

Think:
- quiet closeness
- everyday presence
- recognition
- identity
- affection expressed through routine
- meaningful details

More often than:
- dramatic declarations
- grief-heavy sentiment
- “best gift ever” language
- exaggerated pet-parent jokes

## Quality test

A draft should feel plausible next to:
- modern lifestyle fashion
- editorial product storytelling
- understated pet identity

It should not read like:
- marketplace personalization copy
- novelty merch
- generic AI brand copy
